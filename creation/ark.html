The Ark

Which animals did God say Noah had to take on the Ark?

One pair of each kind of land animal as well as one pair of each kind of flying animal. He also took seven pairs of all kinds of clean animals onboard.

Genesis 6:19-20; Genesis 7:2-3
What is a ‘clean’ animal?

These animals are acceptable for humans to eat, while the ‘unclean’ animals are not. Read about this in Lev 11. Only clean animals were used in sacrifices to God, which is why God told Noah to take 7 extra pairs of ‘clean’ animals onboard as well.

Genesis 7:2-3; Leviticus 11:1-47
How big was the ark?

The Ark was 140m long (longer than a soccer field), 23m wide (as wide as four traffic lanes) and 14m high (higher than a four-story building) and it had three decks.

Genesis 6:14-16
Was the ark stable enough to handle rough seas?

Oh yes! Korean shipbuilders found the Ark, as described in the Bible, to be one of the most stable forms of seafaring vehicles able to handle a very rough sea. According to them, the Ark could withstand winds of up to three-times hurricane-strength and waves up to 30m high (and probably even higher) and it could tilt over beyond 60 degrees without toppling over.

Do you think that Noah built the ark all by himself?

No, his sons also helped and it’s very likely that Noah hired laborers to help them.

How much time had lapsed since the time that God issued the warning that He was going to destroy the earth with a Flood; and why so long?

120 Years. This was a period of grace during which they could repent and turn to God. During this time, Noah built the Ark and continually warned the people: ‘Repent, for a Flood is coming’.

Genesis 6:3; 1 Peter 3:20
What did the people have to do to be saved?

They simply had to believe and trust in God by getting into the Ark when the time came.

Genesis 7:1, 7; Hebrews 11:7
Did they listen? What was their reaction?

No, they mocked Noah, and laughed at him.

Genesis 6:5-13; Matthew 24:37-39
What did Jesus later have to say about this and how is this being repeated today?

Read Matt 24:38-39 first. Jesus has given us His assurance that He is coming to the earth again, this time He will come as The Judge. But just as in the time of Noah, many people simply don’t believe that this will happen and therefore, they will also be caught off guard.

Matthew 24:37-39; 2 Peter 3:3-7
Did Noah have to gather all the animals by himself?

No, Noah could have used hired help or his own servants to gather the animals, just like the emperors did centuries later. Also, the Bible says that God instructed the animals to go into the Ark, so Noah did not have to chase the animals to gather them.

Genesis 6:19-20; Genesis 7:8-9, 15-16
Where was Noah according to Gen 6:20 and 7:15 and what else do we learn here?

He was inside the Ark. We learn that God was completely in control of how the animals were selected and gathered for the Ark. Noah may even have built up an animal collection before the Flood began and then God supernaturally commandeered the animals into the Ark.

Genesis 6:20; Genesis 7:15-16
What advantages could there be to build up an animal collection before the Flood?

Animals in captivity are more used to people and are therefore easier to handle. Noah could have observed the animals carefully to identify any weak or diseased animals and replace them with healthy, strong ones to take on the ark.

Read Gen 7:22. Were there insects on the ark?

Possibly, but Noah did not have to specifically gather them since insects don’t breathe through nostrils.

Genesis 7:21-23
What does it mean when it says that only the ‘original kinds’ were on the ark?

Read page 72-75 in Creation to re-creation or refer to the presentation. The Biblical record (the lists of animals in Leviticus) indicates that the original created kinds were not on the ‘species’ level of today’s classification, but probably on the ‘family’ level, such as the cat family, the elephant family, the dog/wolf family, etc.

Genesis 6:19-20
What do we call a cross-breed between a horse and a donkey?

A mule

What do we call a cross-breed between a zebra and a donkey?

A zonkey

A lion and a tiger? A whale and a dolphin?

A liger and a wholphin

What do we learn from this?

Ligers prove that lions and tigers are different species, but the same kind. Wholpins prove that whales and dolphins are different species, but the same kind. All of these cross-breeds prove that they originated from a specific originally created kind respectively.

Were there poodles and doberman pinchers onboard?

No, because they did not exist yet. Only the original dog/wolf kind was onboard from which the foxes, wolves, jackal, wolves and various dog-breeds later originated

Genesis 6:19-20
Were there dinosaurs onboard and why do you say so?

Yes, because dinosaurs were a land animal kind that breathed air through nostrils and that was created by God together with all the other land animals on Day 6. God told Noah to take one pair of each land animal kind into the Ark. This therefore included dinosaurs.

How on earth did dinosaurs fit into the ark?

Most dinosaurs weren’t that big at all; some were even smaller than chickens! The average size of an adult dinosaur was approximately that of a sheep! (some say a bison). The largest egg ever hatched by a dinosaur was only about the size of a football, which means that even the biggest dinosaurs such as the Apatosaurus and Brachiosaurus used to be quite small, just like you and me!

What advantages were there in taking young animals onboard?

Younger animals are smaller and therefore could have easily fitted into the Ark. They would have been easier to feed and they would have had babies sooner after the Flood.

How many animals were probably on the ark?

Only about 2,000 animals (1,000 pairs) if we use the family level of classification. If we use the generic level of classification, if would be about 16,000 animals (8,000 pairs).

How much space would they have needed on the ark?

Only about 3%

Genesis 6:21
What about food — how much space would it have occupied on the ark?

Read pages 79-81 Creation to re-creation. It would have occupied about 15% of the ark’s space.

Genesis 6:21
What about water?

If they took water onboard as well, the water would probably have occupied about 10% of the space on the Ark. But there’s a large possibility that they may have simply used some of the enormous amounts of rainwater, rather than storing it inside the Ark. Then there would have been even more open space.

What does this all mean?

This means that more than 72% of the Ark and possibly even more, was open space! So there was more than enough space for Noah, his family, all the animals and even the food and water.

Genesis 7:1; 1 Peter 3:20
Who was the open space for?

For all the unbelievers that refused to heed God’s warning.

Genesis 4:2, 17, 21-22
Were the people in Biblical times less clever than we are?

The idea that people of ancient times were less intelligent than we are, is just another evolutionary idea. In Gen 4 it says that Adam and his descendants were grain and livestock farmers. They made musical instruments, could forge metal and built cities. Shortly after the Flood, the Egyptians learnt how to write, how to cut granite and how to build the pyramids with great precision, intricate design and fine detail — all without computers. Adam and Eve were created perfect, but since the fall, the mental ability of humans began to decline over a period of 6,000 years. We are, in all probability far less intelligent than they were.

Genesis 7:11; Genesis 8:13-14
How long were they on the ark

One year and eleven days

Genesis 7:11; Genesis 8:13-14
What happened when God shut the door of the ark, and what can we learn from this?

God gave people time to respond to His warning. Noah acted in faith and entered the ark with his family. Genesis records a seven-day interval before the floodwaters came and tells us that the LORD shut Noah in. Jesus compared the days of Noah with His return. The lesson is to respond to God’s mercy while we have the opportunity, rather than waiting for a crisis. This does not give us a timetable for the final events.

Genesis 6:5-8; Genesis 6:22; Genesis 7:4-16; Matthew 24:37-39; Hebrews 3:15
