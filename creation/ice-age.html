What is ‘topography’?

It is a description of the surface features of the land and this includes mountains, hills and creeks

What do we learn from Psalm 104 regarding earth’s topography?

We learn that God drastically altered earth’s topography

Psalm 104:6-9
What events most likely occurred according to this description in Psalm 104?

The breaking up of the super-continent, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions as well as the relatively rapid movement of earth’s tectonic plates contributed to continental masses and mountains such as the Himalayas, Alps and Andes being pushed up and other areas sinking down under the pressure of the water mass that streamed off the continents into these new ocean basins, exactly as described in Psalm 104.

What are folded mountains and how were they formed?

Folded mountains consist of sedimentary rocks, which means it was deposited by water. They formed when these soft layers were being pushed up resulting in them being distorted, twisted and folded because of this enormous release of energy. Folded mountains must have formed while the sediment layers were still relatively soft, otherwise they would have crumbled and completely disintegrated without forming such even folds.

Could folded mountains have formed because of the eruption of volcanoes?

No, folded mountains consist of sedimentary rocks (it was deposited by water). It is not volcanic rocks (also called igneous rocks). Igneous rocks form when soft, super hot lava cool down and harden.

Do you know of places in South Africa where you can see folded mountains?

Swartberg Mountains

Do all mountains consist of sedimentary rocks?

No, the other mountain type is volcanic mountains, i.o.w., they came into existence because of the eruption of volcanoes.

When were the mountains that we know today formed?

Both volcanic and non-volcanic mountains came into existence more or less during the same time period towards the end of the Flood.

Were the mountains as high before the Flood as they are today?

It couldn’t have been. Mount Everest is almost 9 km’s high, while the Mariana Trench near Guam is almost 11 km’s deep. If it were possible to flatten the earth by evenly leveling the mountains and pushing up the basins, so that the earth was a smooth ball, all of the continents would be covered by water as deep as 2.7 km’s! There is more than enough water around to cover the entire earth — the topography simply needs to be more level. Gen 7:20 states: ‘The waters prevailed fifteen cubits upward, and the mountains were covered’. If the highest mountains above sea level today (9 km’s for Mount Everest) were covered by water that rose seven meters (or 15 cubits) higher than those mountains during the Flood, the world would have been covered with water forever, because the continents would have been too deep under the water. The mountains had to have been lower than they are today and there had to have been geological sinking and upheavals to make room for all that water — and for us.

How does volcanic eruptions affect climatic conditions?

Volcanic eruptions causes pollutants such as dust and ash to be shot up right into the stratosphere (between 16-48 km’s high) where they remain for several years. The presence of massive amounts of these pollutants in the stratosphere can reduce sun radiation by as much as 20% which causes a definite reduction in temperature.

How did volcanic eruptions affect the climate during the Flood?

There are literally thousands of extinct volcanoes around the world and this includes volcanoes under the sea. Their location in the geologic column indicate that they were still active towards the end of the Flood and for a period thereafter while the floodwater receded. Thousands of volcanoes erupted at the same time during the Flood. The huge amounts of dust and ash particles shooting into the stratosphere must have drastically influenced the climatic conditions. The combination of dust, ash and smoke in the stratosphere would have darkened the sky considerably preventing the sun’s rays from reaching the earth and warming it, which is why the landmass must have cooled very rapidly.

Why were the oceans warmer after the Flood than before?

When the fountains of the deep broke open at the beginning of the Flood, causing the oceanic ridges to tear open, super hot lava pushed up through these ridges to form a brand new sizzling hot seafloor. This hot new seafloor caused the ocean waters to be considerably warmer after the Flood than before.

What happens when the oceans are so warm?

Warm oceans cause an increase in evaporation, and because of this a large amount of warm moisture and clouds are formed in the air. When warm air moves over a cold landmass, the warm air cools rapidly.

Why were the landmasses cold after the Flood?

Volcanic dust and ash blocked out the sun’s rays preventing it from warming the landmass. The warm oceans would have caused an increase in moist air resulting in a higher percentage cloud cover which would prevent the sun’s rays to warm the earth. The land surface was stripped bare of vegetation because of the Flood causing most of the sunlight to be deflected away. Because the land surface was so cold, any snow that fell there would not have melted quickly, but rather, it would have remained there to form hard ice cap layers.

What is the ‘recipe’ required for an Ice Age?

A lot of moisture and therefore, an enormous amount of warm water. At the same time, a cold land surface would be required.

When did the Ice Age occur?

The Ice Age must have commenced after the Flood and continued until the oceans cooled considerably and the climate began to stabilize.

How long did the Ice Age last?

Most creation scientists believe that the Ice Age lasted for about 700 years. It took about 500 of the 700 years for the ice caps to build up and 200 of the 700 years to melt down.

How many Ice Ages were there and how much of the earth did the ice cover?

These unique conditions for an Ice Age to develop occurred only once in the history of the world, so there was only one Ice Age. The ice never covered more than one third (1/3) of the earth’s surface.

What do most scientists believe regarding the Ice Age?

What happened when the Ice Age ended?

Many years after the Flood, the oceans cooled down again causing less evaporation, fewer clouds and less snowfall. The volcanoes were no longer as active as during the Flood and eventually all the dust and ash had settled sufficiently for the warm rays of the sun to reheat those parts of the world. The Ice Age had ended and the warmer temperatures caused sheets of ice to begin sliding southwards from the North Pole, off the continents. Scientists refer to this as the Glacial Period.

How do we know glaciers were larger than today?

When these ice sheets or glaciers slowly slide down steep slopes, they grind and crush the rock material beneath and carve out U-shaped valleys while dragging the crushed rock material along over long distances. Later, after the glacier had melted, you can clearly see the grooves in the hard rock. This is how we know that there were previously much larger ice sheets present than today.

How did people and animals distribute to other continents?

During the Ice Age the sea level was much lower than today because so much water had been locked into the ice cover. Consequently, for a long time, land bridges would have been available whereby people and animals could have moved to other continents. Smaller animals could also have crossed the sea on rafts consisting of pumice or floating vegetation.
